How this works. Each starter is converted to a regressed RA/9 from FIP components (HR, BB, K) blended with ERA and pulled toward league average by sample size, then combined with that team's bullpen for the innings the starter is not projected to cover. Offenses are park-neutralized season run rates nudged by 3-week form. The two run expectations are simulated 30,000 times with a negative-binomial run distribution to produce win, run-line and total probabilities. League baseline: 4.47 R/G, 4.17 ERA.

Fair odds are model prices, not book prices. Where a real sportsbook line appears above, it was read from a named source. Lines are never estimated — a blank means no line was found, because a guessed line is worse than no line.

There is no lock tier here, on purpose. On the companion board a lock means 88% — tennis at 72%+ and soccer favourites. Baseball's ceiling is about 61%: above 55% this model goes 133-84 (61.3%) across 217 backtested picks, and the cells that look better (65%+ at 75%, 66%+ at 87.5%) rest on 12 and 8 picks with a 50.0% band directly beneath them. A badge promising more than 61% would be selling a sample as a rate.

Hit rate is not profit. On the companion board's 140 priced graded picks, the ones whose model probability beat the de-vigged price went 41-45 (47.7%) for +10.4% ROI, while the ones that lost to the price went 35-19 (64.8%) for +3.9%. The worse record made more money because it was paid better. So the price is frozen here alongside each pick and the record carries ROI, not just wins.
